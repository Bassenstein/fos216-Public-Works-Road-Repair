03

Treatment Method & Decision Matrix

Each grade band maps to the most cost-effective treatment. Preventive infrared work protects streets that are still sound; repaving rehabilitates the middle; saw-cut reconstruction is reserved for full structural failures — the method of last resort and the most expensive per square yard.

A

Crack Seal / Preventive Hold

Excellent pavement (PCR 91–100). Seal cracks before water intrusion begins; no surface treatment required this cycle. Lowest cost-per-mile in the program.

PREVENTIVE · SEALANT
B

Infrared Spot Repair — Potholes & Cracks

Good pavement (PCR 81–90). Kasi Infrared Patriot heats, scarifies and recompacts distressed patches with no cold joints — a seamless, waterproof repair that keeps a B street from slipping to C. Only the distressed fraction of the surface is treated.

INFRARED · CORRECTIVE-PREVENTIVE
C

Infrared Repair (Heavier) → Repave Watchlist

Fair pavement (PCR 71–80). Infrared remains viable for potholes, utility-cut and edge repair; segments trending toward D are flagged for mill-and-overlay in the next cycle.

INFRARED
D

Mill & Repave — Full-Width Overlay

Poor pavement (PCR 61–70). Structurally sound base but a failing surface: repave the full segment area. This is your adjustable $/SY repave rate in the model below.

REPAVE · $/SY ADJUSTABLE
F

Saw-Cut, Remove & Replace — Full-Depth Reconstruction

Failed pavement (PCR ≤ 60). Base course has lost support; surface treatment would fail within a season. Saw-cut to full depth, rebuild base, and repave. Longest-lived repair (10–15+ yrs) and the highest unit cost.

RECONSTRUCTION · FULL-DEPTH
